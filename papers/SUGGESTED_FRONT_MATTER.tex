% Suggested title
\title{Template Priors in Small CNNs: Kernel Alignment, Learning Dynamics,
and Intervention-Budget Sensitivity}

% Suggested abstract
\begin{abstract}
Recognizable first-layer kernels are often treated as evidence of
interpretability, but weight-space resemblance does not establish how a
network uses its features. We test this distinction in two controlled
rendering tasks and two small CNNs using template initialization, continued
template retention, and gradual release. In an initial factorial study,
retention strongly increases alignment to edge, corner and ring templates,
but does not yield a Holm-corrected improvement in four prespecified
selected-channel patching contrasts. With longer training, template
initialization is not uniformly faster, and releasing retention improves
compositional accuracy relative to constant retention while reducing kernel
alignment. Reanalysis of the final checkpoints shows that the
release-minus-retention difference in selected-channel patching depends on
intervention budget: for TinyCNN on the compositional task it is negative
when one or two channels are patched and positive when four or eight are
patched under all three validation-only rankings; TwoLayerCNN is also
ranking- and budget-sensitive. One-to-one template matching across saved
checkpoint states preserves the morphology contrast, ruling out repeated
nearest-template reuse as its explanation. These results separate kernel
morphology from unit selectivity and intervention behavior, and show that
conclusions about a template prior can depend on how functional use is
operationalized. The budget analysis is exploratory and does not establish
human interpretability, causal identifiability, or generality beyond the
tested models and synthetic tasks.
\end{abstract}

% Suggested replacement for the final contribution paragraph in the Introduction.
% The intent is to make the claim ladder explicit and reduce defensive repetition.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we separate weight-space template
alignment from independently evaluated single-unit concept selectivity,
localization and matched internal interventions in a controlled setting.
Second, we distinguish initialization from persistent constraint by comparing
continued retention with gradual release over a longer optimization horizon.
Third, we show that the retention--release comparison under selected-channel
patching is not invariant to intervention budget: on the compositional task,
the shallow-model difference reverses between small and larger channel sets
under three validation-only rankings. The morphology contrast persists under
one-to-one kernel matching. Within the tested setting, these results establish
that retained template structure and functional intervention measurements can
diverge. The budget-dependent patching result is exploratory; we do not claim
that release redistributes causal information, that retained kernels are
human-interpretable by construction, or that the measured intervention
identifies a unique mechanism.
